# Short video production

Use this workflow for original, agent-reviewed short videos. Ask for the intended audience only when it is genuinely unclear; never imply an example reel grants rights to reuse its footage, script, voice, or music.

The production agent does the research, writing, asset sourcing, editing and quality checks; do not delegate routine manual work to the user. AI judgments and technical checks are not proof of factual, transcription, pronunciation, visual or licensing accuracy. If a necessary check cannot be done reliably, disclose the blocker instead of calling the output finished. Preserve the user's explicit final-export approval.

1. Define the topic, audience, platform, duration, and budget. Research and fact-check claims. Write an original script, not a close paraphrase of a book or reel. Avoid promising investment returns. Do not send private text to external providers without permission.
2. Show a storyboard before production: for each beat include time, spoken words, an actual moving shot, motion or transition, caption/highlight, optional sound, and source/license. Favor visual storytelling, pacing, varied shot sizes, and meaningful transitions; static cards or animated icons alone are not a substitute for cinematic footage. Confirm licensing, reuse rights, creator attribution, costs, and likeness restrictions. Do not promise that stock or AI tools are free or available.
3. Configure `examples/narrated-video.json` or a copy: each scene may use `visual.type: stock` with `media: video`, or `generated` with `media: video` and an explicitly configured generator command. For a provider, confirm its actual API, credentials, cost, terms, and output before use. The project does not ship an AI-video model.
4. Configure optional Nepali narration. `prepare` generates it along with the configured visual assets; do not run `fetch`, `generate` or `narrate` separately first because `prepare` refuses to overwrite assets. Narration sends scene text to Microsoft's online service; use only with permission, and check its terms. The default is `ne-NP-HemkalaNeural`; verify pronunciation, timing, and quality. The renderer extends scenes to avoid truncating speech.
5. Use `prepare` to acquire configured moving visuals and generate narration after presenting the full storyboard. It validates streams and checks provider word boundaries against the script and audio, but does not judge visual quality or automatically edit. Use HyperFrames' editable HTML starter for motion experiments; it is not an automatic conversion from a Katha project or a substitute for footage. For supplied speech, `transcribe` uses local whisper.cpp and produces an unverified draft only; never use it as final captions or ask the user to repair it. If no reliable verification method is available, disclose that accurate raw-speech captions cannot be guaranteed. For original narration the renderer burns word-timed white captions with the active word in yellow. Target 1080 × 1920 at 30 fps and keep text within x 65–916, y 270–1250; inspect actual glyph bounds, contrast and platform overlays.
6. Only after the user explicitly says `final export गर`, run `python -m katha render <project> --approval "final export गर"` or perform a final HyperFrames export. Do not write to `Exports` beforehand. Watch the complete output with audio. Check actual motion, visual relevance, pronunciation, caption synchronization, clipping, duration, factual claims, and attribution. Record stock provenance and provider costs. Disclose missing assets, credentials, or failed generation rather than substituting a slideshow and calling it an AI video. Do not publish unchecked output.

For optional raw-footage edits, transcription, cuts, punch-ins, badges, motion graphics, music and sound effects require human editing. For a before/after comparison, use real source and edited footage; show the voiceover script first and storyboard before the export gate. Neither face-touch detection nor ElevenLabs is integrated.

Consult `docs/brand-and-production.md` for the proposed channel direction and production standards. Treat the brand name, logo, and styling as a proposal until approved. For commands, configuration details, dependencies, and limitations, see `docs/usage.md`.
